Behaviour Support Information

This information may help staff recognize conditions that can increase the likelihood of Behaviour. Record what was actually observed in the ABC form.

Setting Events may influence Behaviour even when they happened earlier. Antecedents happen immediately before the Behaviour. Record observable facts and do not guess motives. A Setting Event does not prove the cause of Behaviour.

What are Setting Events?

A setting event is an aspect of the person’s health, environment, social situation, or routine that may affect whether Behaviour occurs. It may have happened hours earlier and does not necessarily occur immediately before the Behaviour.

Physiological / Biological Setting Events

  • Not having enough sleep or sleeping poorly during the night.
  • Changes to eating routines or diet, including missing a meal or consuming too much sugar.
  • Reaction to lactose or dairy products, which may contribute to sinus infections or constipation.
  • Not feeling well, including stomach ache, tooth pain, headache, or sinus infection.
  • Difficulty having regular bowel movements, including constipation or megacolon.
  • Hunger or thirst.

Physical / Environmental Setting Events

  • Sensitivity to bright natural sunlight, including covering the eyes.
  • Loud or unexpected noises in the environment.
  • Becoming overheated or having wet clothing or shoes, which may lead to removing clothing.
  • Becoming too cold, including covering with a blanket or having difficulty sleeping.
  • Neville has preferred seating arrangements and may become unsettled when expected seating patterns change.
  • Not being able to receive deep pressure.
  • A high level of activity or clutter in the environment.
  • Lack of predictability in daily routines.
  • Activities involving high amounts of language.
  • Transitioning from a preferred activity to a non-preferred activity.
  • Participating in non-preferred or quiet activities for long periods.
  • Not receiving sufficient access to movement activities or movement breaks.
  • Unfamiliar or difficult activities.

Social / Interpersonal Setting Events

  • Being refused a request or activity.
  • Being disciplined or reprimanded.
  • Not receiving enough time to transition from a preferred activity.
  • Changes in staffing, including regular staff being away or on break.
  • Changes in the daily schedule or routine without warning.
  • Difficulty interacting effectively with others.
  • Limited positive peer interaction.
  • Hygiene routines, including support after a bowel movement or nail cutting.

Fast Triggers — Antecedents

An antecedent is something that happens directly before the challenging Behaviour and may predict that the Behaviour will occur.

  • Being told to wait.
  • Being reprimanded.
  • Loud or unexpected noises, such as a truck horn.
  • Not receiving help immediately after requesting it.
  • Not being able to access a preferred activity or food item.
  • Hearing another person become angry or upset.

ABC Recording Reminder

Before the Behaviour (A)
Record what happened immediately before the Behaviour.

Behaviour Observed (B)
Record only what was actually seen or heard.

Staff Response (C)
Record what staff actually did.

Outcome
Record the duration until calm, how the client calmed down, and any additional relevant observations.

Do not guess motives or assume that a Setting Event caused the Behaviour.
